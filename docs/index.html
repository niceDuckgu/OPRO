OPRO Orthogonal Panel-Relative Operators for Panel-Aware In-Context Image Generation

Sanghyeon LeeMinwoo LeeEuijin ShinKangyeol KimSeunghwan ChoiJaegul Choo

Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST)

Paper arXiv Supplementary Code BibTeX

In-context image generation tiles reference images and a target into panels, yet attention in a diffusion transformer has no signal for whether two tokens share a panel. OPRO rotates the queries and keys of each panel with its own learnable orthogonal operator. Attention within a panel stays exactly as the pre-trained backbone computes it, and only attention across panels is adapted.

Abstract

We introduce a parameter-efficient adaptation method for panel-aware in-context image generation with pre-trained diffusion transformers. The key idea is to compose learnable, panel-specific orthogonal operators onto the backbone’s frozen positional encodings. This design provides two desirable properties: (1) isometry, which preserves the geometry of internal features, and (2) same-panel invariance, which maintains the model’s pre-trained intra-panel synthesis behavior. Through controlled experiments, we demonstrate that the effectiveness of our adaptation method is not tied to a specific positional encoding design but generalizes across diverse positional encoding regimes. By enabling effective panel-relative conditioning, the proposed method consistently improves in-context image-based instructional editing pipelines, including state-of-the-art approaches.

Method

Let \(\tilde{q}_i\) and \(\tilde{k}_j\) be the queries and keys of a frozen backbone after its own positional encoding, and let \(p(i)\) be the panel of token \(i\). OPRO learns one operator \(U_p \in \mathrm{SO}(d_h)\) per panel and applies it to every query and key of that panel as

\[ \hat{q}_i = U_{p(i)}\,\tilde{q}_i, \qquad \hat{k}_j = U_{p(j)}\,\tilde{k}_j, \]

so the attention logit becomes

\[ s'_{ij} \;=\; \frac{\tilde{q}_i^{\top}\,\bigl(U_{p(i)}^{\top} U_{p(j)}\bigr)\,\tilde{k}_j}{\sqrt{d_h}}. \]

The relative operator \(U_{p(i)}^{\top}U_{p(j)}\) collapses to the identity when both tokens lie in the same panel, and it is a learned rotation otherwise. The formulation only assumes position-aware queries and keys, so it applies to absolute encodings and to rotary encodings such as RoPE, LieRE, and ComRoPE.

Isometry

\( \lVert \hat{q}_i \rVert = \lVert \tilde{q}_i \rVert, \quad \lVert \hat{k}_j \rVert = \lVert \tilde{k}_j \rVert \)

The operators rotate features without rescaling them, which avoids unintended changes in the magnitude of attention logits.

Same-panel invariance

\( p(i) = p(j) \;\Rightarrow\; s'_{ij} = s_{ij} \)

Attention within a panel is identical to that of the pre-trained backbone, so the adapter spends its capacity on cross-panel interaction.

Zero-interference initialization

\( L_p = 0,\; R_p \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2) \;\Rightarrow\; U_p = I \)

Training starts from the unmodified backbone, and the gradient with respect to \(L_p\) is non-zero from the first step.

Each operator is parameterized in the Lie algebra with two low-rank factors \(L_p, R_p \in \mathbb{R}^{d_h \times \rho}\) as

\[ A_p = L_p R_p^{\top} - R_p L_p^{\top}, \qquad U_p = \exp(A_p), \]

which is orthogonal by construction and trains with a standard optimizer. On FLUX with two panels and rank \(\rho = 32\), the operators add 0.93M parameters across 57 attention layers of head dimension \(d_h = 128\).

Two-stage compositional reasoning benchmark

A synthetic benchmark isolates the two abilities that in-context generation requires, namely perception within a panel and reasoning across panels. Stage 1 trains a ViT-B from scratch to classify the sum of two arrow orientations in a single panel. Stage 2 freezes this backbone and fine-tunes adapters on an \(n \times n\) grid in which every row follows a hidden rule, either a rotation by a fixed multiple of 45° or a mirror symmetry. The model must infer the rule from the context rows and predict the missing panel.

The paper compares a linear probe, LoRA, and LoRA with OPRO on backbones with four positional encodings (APE, RoPE, LieRE, and ComRoPE) and three grid sizes, and it ablates isometry, same-panel invariance, and the initialization. The results are reported in Tables 1 and 2 of the paper. The data generator and the training code are part of the release.

Instructional image editing

Instructional editing is a two-panel task with a source panel and a target panel. OPRO is added with rank 32 to four in-context pipelines. ICEdit, ACE++, and InsertAnything build on FLUX.1 Fill and encode both panels on a global canvas, and UNO builds on FLUX.1 and encodes each panel in its own frame. All comparisons use the MagicBrush test set, and the quantitative results are in Table 3 of the paper.

Code

The repository contains the operator module, an attention processor that attaches it to FLUX transformers in diffusers, and the two-stage benchmark. 

# any attention layer, after its positional encoding
from opro import OPROLieLowRank

opro = OPROLieLowRank(num_panels=2, head_dim=128, rank=32)
q, k = opro.apply_to_qk(q, k, panel_ids)   # q, k: [batch, heads, tokens, head_dim]

# FLUX transformers in diffusers
from opro import add_opro_to_flux, grid_panel_ids

opro = add_opro_to_flux(pipe.transformer, num_panels=2, rank=32)
opro.set_panel_ids(grid_panel_ids(32, 64, panel_rows=1, panel_cols=2))
