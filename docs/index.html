OPRO Orthogonal Panel-Relative Operators for Panel-Aware In-Context Image Generation

Sanghyeon LeeMinwoo LeeEuijin ShinKangyeol KimSeunghwan ChoiJaegul Choo

Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST)

Paper arXiv Supplementary Code BibTeX

A 0.93M-parameter adapter that makes the attention of a diffusion transformer panel-aware and improves in-context image editing.

Task

In-context image generation

Reference images and a target are tiled as panels, and a diffusion transformer generates the target panel. Instructional editing is the two-panel case.

Problem

Attention is panel-agnostic

Attention receives no signal for whether two tokens share a panel. One adapter must learn the transfer across panels and preserve the synthesis within a panel.

OPRO

One orthogonal operator per panel

The operator rotates the queries and keys of its panel. Attention within a panel stays exactly as pre-trained, and only attention across panels is adapted.

Method

OPRO learns an orthogonal operator \(U_p\) for each panel \(p\) and applies it to the position-aware queries and keys of a frozen backbone. The attention logit between tokens \(i\) and \(j\) becomes

\[ s'_{ij} \;=\; \frac{\tilde{q}_i^{\top}\,\bigl(U_{p(i)}^{\top} U_{p(j)}\bigr)\,\tilde{k}_j}{\sqrt{d_h}}. \]
  • Same-panel invariance. For two tokens of one panel, \(U_p^{\top}U_p = I\) and the logit equals that of the pre-trained backbone.
  • Isometry. The operators preserve query and key norms, so attention logits are not rescaled.
  • Zero-interference initialization. \(U_p = \exp(L_p R_p^{\top} - R_p L_p^{\top})\) with \(L_p = 0\), so training starts from the unmodified backbone.

Abstract

We introduce a parameter-efficient adaptation method for panel-aware in-context image generation with pre-trained diffusion transformers. The key idea is to compose learnable, panel-specific orthogonal operators onto the backbone’s frozen positional encodings. This design provides two desirable properties: (1) isometry, which preserves the geometry of internal features, and (2) same-panel invariance, which maintains the model’s pre-trained intra-panel synthesis behavior. Through controlled experiments, we demonstrate that the effectiveness of our adaptation method is not tied to a specific positional encoding design but generalizes across diverse positional encoding regimes. By enabling effective panel-relative conditioning, the proposed method consistently improves in-context image-based instructional editing pipelines, including state-of-the-art approaches.
